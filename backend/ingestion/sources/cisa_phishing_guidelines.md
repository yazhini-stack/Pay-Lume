# CISA Security Tip (ST04-014): Recognizing and Reporting Phishing and Social Engineering

## Overview
Phishing is a form of social engineering where attackers deceive victims into revealing sensitive information, deploying malware, or transferring funds. Attackers often masquerade as reputable companies, financial institutions, government agencies, or trusted colleagues.

## Key Indicators of Phishing Communications
1. **Urgency and Coercion**: Messages demanding immediate action ("Account suspended within 24 hours", "Unauthorized charge detected, click immediately to cancel"). Attackers rely on anxiety to bypass critical scrutiny.
2. **Generic Greetings**: Salutations such as "Dear Valued Customer" rather than your specific account name.
3. **Mismatched Sender Domains**: The display name may read "PayPal Security" or "Bank Fraud Department", but the underlying email address or SMS shortcode originates from an unrelated domain or personal webmail account.
4. **Suspicious Hyperlinks**: Hovering over or inspecting links reveals URLs pointing to unrecognized hostnames, excessive subdomains (e.g., `paypal.com.account-verify.online`), or URL shorteners masking the final destination.
5. **Requests for Credentials or Confidential Information**: Legitimate financial institutions and service providers will never send unsolicited emails or SMS messages asking you to provide or "verify" passwords, PINs, OTP codes, or full card details.

## Verification Best Practices
- Never click directly on links inside unverified messages.
- Navigate to the official website by typing the known URL directly into your web browser or accessing your provider's official mobile application.
- Contact customer support using verified phone numbers listed on the back of your debit/credit card or on your billing statements.
- Report phishing emails to the Anti-Phishing Working Group (APWG) and federal authorities.
